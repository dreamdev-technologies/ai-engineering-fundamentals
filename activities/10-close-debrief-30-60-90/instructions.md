# 10. Close: debrief and 30-60-90

Whole room, then individual. 30 minutes.

## Goal

Turn the session into commitments. The debrief connects what you did back to the principles; the 30-60-90 makes each person name what changes, with dates.

## Steps

1. **Questions board, 5 min.** Walk the board. Anything answered along the way is ticked off out loud; anything still open gets an owner or an honest "not answerable here".
2. **Debrief against the principles, 10 min.** Four principles on screen (`principles.md` in this directory): set up for flow, build quality in, automate validation, keep batches small. For each, someone names a moment in the exercises you ran where that principle did real work, or where its absence hurt. The equation under all four: delegation you can extend equals verification you have built.
3. **Back to the value stream, 5 min.** Look again at the maps from activity 02, and the wait each group circled. Given what you now know, where in your stream would delegation with verification actually shorten lead time? Mark it.
4. **Write your 30-60-90, 7 min, individually.** Three lines: in 30 days, one thing you will have started (small, in your own control); by 60 days, one practice your team runs routinely; by 90 days, one result someone else could check. Write it as things done, not intentions.
5. **One line each, 3 min.** Around the room: each person reads out one of their three lines. No discussion; the point is public commitment.

## Done when

The board is cleared or owned, and every person leaves with a written 30-60-90 and has said one line of it aloud.

## Notes

Keep the repository: everything in it is reusable on real work. A useful default 30-day line for a team like this is characterisation tests under one piece of the legacy estate; it is the smallest step with the biggest unlock. A second, cheaper one: put the test grader from activity 08 on every pull request, whoever or whatever wrote the tests.
